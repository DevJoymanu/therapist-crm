Chapter 1

Getting Started

Therapist CRM is a secure, web-based tool designed specifically for clinical professionals. You access it through any modern web browser — no app installation required.

Logging In

Figure 1.1 — The login screen is the entry point to Therapist CRM

1
Enter your username and password provided by your clinic administrator.
2
Click Sign In. You will be taken directly to your personalised dashboard.
Tip: Your session stays active while your browser is open. Always click Logout (via your name in the top-right) when using a shared computer.

Understanding the Interface Layout

The workspace is divided into three persistent areas that are always visible on desktop:

Figure 1.2 — Desktop layout: Sidebar (left) + Top bar (top) + Main content (centre)

① Sidebar

Fixed left panel with all five main navigation links. Click any section name to navigate.

② Top bar

Always-on header with a global patient search, an Add session shortcut, and your user menu.

③ Content area

The main workspace — pages load here instantly without a full browser refresh.

On a phone or tablet: The sidebar is replaced by a bottom navigation bar (five icons at the foot of the screen) and a ☰ menu button in the top-left corner that opens a slide-in drawer with all navigation links.

Responding to Alerts

The Alerts panel on your dashboard is the most clinically important area. Any patient whose session produces a High risk score appears here automatically.

Figure 2.2 — The Alerts panel shows patients requiring urgent clinical review

  1. Spot a patient name in the Alerts panel on the dashboard.

  2. Click the row. The system opens the specific session that triggered the alert, showing the full clinical note and score breakdown.

  3. Review the session notes, interventions recorded, and the scoring algorithm to understand why the risk flag was raised.

  4. Take clinical action: contact the patient, update your notes, or schedule an urgent follow-up appointment.

Important: Alerts are generated automatically based on the scoring criteria you have configured. They are a clinical aid, not a substitute for your professional judgement. Always review the full session context before acting.

  3. Click Save patient. The system takes you directly to the new patient's profile page.

Figure 3.2 — The Add Patient form

Required fields: First name and last name are the only required fields. All other fields (email, phone, emergency contact, date of birth) are optional but recommended for complete clinical records.

Chapter 5

Session Notes & Scoring

Session notes are the core clinical record in Therapist CRM. After each patient session, you create a structured note that captures what happened and enter numerical scores on each of your configured clinical measures. The system then calculates wellness and risk scores automatically.

Creating a Session Note — Step by Step

  1. Click Add session in the top bar, or open a patient profile and click the blue Add session button there.

  2. Complete the Session details section (patient, date, clinical text fields).

  3. Enter numerical scores in the Scores section for each clinical measure.

  4. Click Save session. Scores are calculated and the patient's risk level updates immediately.

Figure 5.1 — The Session Note form, Part 1: Clinical details

1
Patient & linked appointment — Select the patient first; the appointment dropdown then filters to show only that patient's scheduled appointments. Linking is optional but useful for tracking session coverage.
2
Presenting concerns & interventions — Freetext fields for your clinical narrative. Write as much or as little as your workflow requires. These appear in the session detail view and in the patient's Session Notes tab.
3
Treatment plan vs. private notes — The Plan field is the patient-facing treatment plan and homework. Private notes is for confidential clinical observations — a reminder to yourself, supervisor notes, or safeguarding flags.

How the Algorithm Works

After you save a session, the system processes your raw scores into two summary metrics automatically:

Figure 5.3 — The scoring algorithm: raw values → normalised → direction-adjusted → weighted into wellness and risk scores

Key insight: A high wellness score (closer to 10) is good — it means the patient is doing well. A high risk score (closer to 10) is a concern. The system automatically flags patients when risk exceeds the threshold configured in Settings.

Chapter 6

Tracking Patient Progress Over Time

The most powerful feature of Therapist CRM is longitudinal progress tracking. Every session note you create adds a data point to the patient's progress chart, letting you and your patient see treatment working in real time.

Accessing the Progress Chart

  1. Open a patient's profile (click their name anywhere in the system).

  2. Click the Progress Tracking tab in the right-hand panel.

  3. The chart loads automatically, plotting wellness and risk scores across all recorded sessions.

Figure 6.1 — Progress chart for Sarah Mitchell: wellness improving steadily (blue ↑), risk declining (red - - -)

Reading the Chart

Blue line (Wellness)

A rising blue line means the patient's overall wellbeing is improving. This is the primary indicator of treatment effectiveness.

Red dashed line (Risk)

A falling red line means risk is decreasing — the patient is becoming less vulnerable. A sudden spike needs immediate attention.

Data points

Each dot represents one session. The more consistent your recording, the more informative the chart becomes over time.

Clinical use: Print or screen-share the progress chart during patient reviews to make treatment progress visible and tangible. Many patients find seeing their own improvement highly motivating.

Chapter 7

Settings & Scoring Criteria

Click Settings in the sidebar to manage the clinical scoring measures used throughout the system. Each criterion defines one clinical scale — its name, numerical range, direction (higher = worse or higher = better), and weighting in the overall wellness score.

Administrator task: Scoring criteria should be configured by a clinical lead or system administrator before therapists begin entering session notes. Changing criteria after sessions have been recorded will affect how historical scores appear in charts.

Figure 7.1 — The Scoring Criteria settings page

Understanding Each Field

Min / Max scoreThe valid numerical range for this scale. Values outside this range are flagged in the session form.0 to 27

Adding a custom measure: You can create any scale your clinic uses. Click Add criterion, enter the name, range, direction, and weight. The new scale immediately appears in the session note form for all subsequent sessions.

Glossary of Key Terms

Wellness score — A calculated score from 0–10 summarising patient wellbeing across all measures. Higher is better.

Risk score — A calculated score from 0–10 indicating clinical risk. Higher scores trigger alerts.

Criterion — One clinical measuring instrument (e.g. PHQ-9, GAD-7). Configured in Settings.

Session note — A structured clinical record created after each patient session, containing narrative text and numerical scores.

Alert — An automatic flag on the dashboard when a patient's risk score exceeds the configured threshold.

Normalise — The process of converting raw scores to a 0–1 scale for fair comparison across different measures with different ranges.

Weight — The proportion (%) a measure contributes to the overall wellness score. All weights should total 100%.

Active patient — A patient currently receiving treatment. Inactive patients are archived but not deleted.

Need help? Contact your clinic administrator or system manager. This guide covers standard usage — your administrator can help with account issues, data exports, and configuration changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

FAQs — Part 1

Common questions from therapists using Therapist CRM, grouped by topic.

Login & Accounts

What do I do if I forget my password?

Contact your clinic administrator or IT manager — they can reset your password from the admin panel. There is no self-service password reset link on the login screen. Once reset, log in with the temporary password provided and update it immediately.

Can multiple therapists share one account?

No — never share login credentials. Each therapist must have their own account. Shared accounts make it impossible to trace who recorded a session note or updated a patient record, which creates clinical and legal risk. Ask your administrator to create individual accounts for each clinician.

Why was I logged out automatically?

Your session may have expired after a period of inactivity, or another tab may have triggered a logout. Simply log in again — your data is not lost. If this happens frequently, ask your administrator about adjusting the session timeout setting.

How do I log out securely on a shared computer?

Click your name or initials in the top-right corner of any page, then click Logout. Always do this before leaving a shared device. Do not rely on simply closing the browser tab — the session may remain active in the background.

Patients

Can I permanently delete a patient record?

Yes, but with caution. Open the patient profile, click Edit, and use the delete option at the bottom. Deletion is permanent — all associated appointments, session notes, and scores are removed and cannot be recovered. For patients who have completed treatment, it is better to deactivate them (uncheck the "Active patient" box) so their records are preserved for audit purposes.

How do I mark a patient as inactive without deleting their records?

Open the patient profile → click Edit → uncheck the Active patient checkbox → click Save. The patient disappears from the active care roster but all their session notes, appointments, and progress data remain intact and fully searchable.

I searched for a patient but they don't appear — what's wrong?

  • Confirm the spelling — the search matches exactly what you type.
  • The patient may be marked as inactive. Try the "All" filter tab on the Patients page.
  • The patient may not have been added yet — use Add Patient to create a new record.
  • If you believe the record exists but cannot find it, contact your administrator.

Can I export patient data to a spreadsheet or external system?

There is no built-in export in the current version. For audit or transfer purposes, contact your system administrator — they can run a database export through the admin panel. Do not manually copy patient data to unapproved systems, as this may breach your clinic's data governance policy.

Appointments

Can I set up recurring (repeating) appointments?

Not automatically in the current version. Appointments must be scheduled individually. A practical workaround: immediately after finishing a session, schedule the next appointment before leaving the system — this takes under a minute and keeps your schedule consistently up to date.

A patient did not attend — what should I do in the system?

Open the appointment, click Reschedule, change the Status field to No-show, and save. This keeps an accurate attendance record without creating a session note for a session that did not occur. Use the Internal notes field to document any attempted contact.

Can I link a session note to an appointment after it has been saved?

Yes. Open the session note (Reports → click the row → Edit), then select the appointment from the Linked appointment dropdown and save. The link is established retrospectively without affecting any clinical content or scores already recorded.

FAQs — Part 2

Session Notes & Scoring

What if I forget to enter scores during a session note?

Scores are optional per session. The note saves with all your clinical text regardless. However, no wellness or risk calculation will be generated for that session and it will not appear on the progress chart. You can go back and add scores later by clicking Edit — they are calculated and integrated into the chart immediately on saving.

Can I edit or correct a session note after saving it?

Yes. Go to Reports → click the session row → click Edit. All fields — clinical text, scores, and the linked appointment — can be updated. Wellness and risk scores are recalculated automatically on saving. There is no change history log, so corrections are made directly to the original record. If your clinic requires an audit trail, document changes in your own records outside the system.

Why is a patient suddenly showing as "High risk" when they seemed fine last week?

A high-risk flag triggers automatically when the risk score from the most recent session exceeds the configured threshold. Possible reasons:

  • Genuine clinical deterioration — review the session scores and narrative carefully.
  • Data entry error — an unusually high or low score on one measure can push the overall risk score over the threshold. Check each score against the patient's presentation.
  • Criteria change — if weights or thresholds were recently updated, historical classifications may shift. Check with your administrator.

Always apply your clinical judgement — the system is an aid, not a diagnosis.

A score is flagged as "outside the expected range" — what does that mean?

Each criterion has a defined minimum and maximum (e.g. PHQ-9: 0–27). If you enter a value outside that range, the score card is highlighted in amber. The system still saves the session, but out-of-range scores are excluded from the wellness and risk calculation. Double-check the figure and correct it if it was a typing error.

Do I need to complete every scoring criterion for every session?

No. Only enter scores for measures that are clinically appropriate for that session. A blank criterion simply does not contribute to the calculation. For example, if a session focused on crisis planning and a full PHQ-9 was not administered, leave that field blank rather than entering an estimate.

Why doesn't the wellness score match the raw number I entered?

The wellness score (0–10) is not the same as a raw score. The system normalises each raw value against its scale range, adjusts for direction (higher = better or higher = worse), applies configured weights, and averages into a single metric. See Chapter 5 — Clinical Scoring Explained (page 11) for the full algorithm walkthrough.

Progress Tracking

Why is the progress chart empty for a patient?

The chart only plots sessions where at least one numerical score was recorded. If all score fields were left blank, no data points appear. Edit at least one existing session to add scores, or ensure scores are entered in future sessions to start building the chart.

How many sessions are needed before the chart shows a useful trend?

A trend line becomes meaningful from around 3–4 data points. With fewer than 3 sessions the chart plots the points but it is too early to draw clinical conclusions. Consistent recording from the first session creates the richest longitudinal picture over time.

Can I share the progress chart with my patient?

The most practical approach is to open the patient's Progress Tracking tab on a shared screen during the session. There is no built-in export button. Alternatively, use your OS screenshot tool (Win+Shift+S on Windows, ⌘+Shift+4 on Mac) to capture the chart and paste it into a document or patient letter.

Technical & Data

Which browsers are supported?

Up-to-date versions of Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Firefox, and Safari. Internet Explorer is not supported. Chrome or Edge are recommended, especially when printing this guide to PDF.

Does the system work on a phone or tablet?

Yes. Therapist CRM is fully mobile-responsive. On phones and tablets you will see a bottom navigation bar (five icons at the foot of the screen) and a ☰ menu button in the top-left corner. Patient lists, appointments, and session notes display as touch-friendly cards rather than scrollable tables.

A page is loading slowly or won't load — what should I do?

  • Check your internet connection — the system requires a live server connection.
  • Refresh the page (F5 or Ctrl+R).
  • Clear your browser cache (Ctrl+Shift+Delete) and reload.
  • Try a different browser.
  • If the problem persists, the server may be temporarily unavailable — contact your administrator.

Is patient data secure and confidential?

Security depends on your clinic's deployment. Confirm with your administrator that the application is served over HTTPS, the database is on a secure access-controlled server, regular backups are in place, and access is restricted to authorised staff only. Never use the system over public Wi-Fi without a VPN, and never leave a logged-in session unattended.

Can I undo an accidental deletion?

There is no undo function. Deletions are permanent once confirmed. If you accidentally delete a record, contact your administrator immediately — depending on the backup schedule, restoration from a recent database backup may be possible. The system always shows a confirmation prompt before deleting anything precisely to prevent accidental loss.

Can I use Therapist CRM as the sole record for a patient's clinical care?

Therapist CRM is designed to support clinical documentation, not to replace formal record-keeping obligations. Check your regulatory body's requirements (e.g. BACP, UKCP, BPS in the UK; APA, NASW in the US) for what constitutes an adequate clinical record. You may need to maintain additional formal records alongside this system.
